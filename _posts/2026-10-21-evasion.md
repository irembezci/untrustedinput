Evasion is an approach that changes an input to make it harder for security controls to detect or block. In AI security, it can help assess whether filters and other safeguards behave consistently across different ways of expressing the same intent.

Evasion is not necessarily an attack goal by itself. It is often a method for making another technique harder to recognize.

## Common approaches

### Obfuscation

Changing the form of an expression to make its meaning harder for automated controls to recognize.

### Encoding

Representing content in a different form. During an authorized assessment, observe how the system handles that representation and where it is decoded.

### Semantic Evasion

Keeping the underlying meaning while changing the wording or phrasing. Evaluation focuses on how the system interprets the request, rather than on specific surface words.

### Multilingual Evasion

Assessing whether inputs in different languages are handled consistently by security controls.

## Evaluation note

A difference in how a control responds to different phrasing does not, by itself, demonstrate security impact. Define the scope and expected behavior, record results in controlled conditions, and test only systems you are authorized to assess.
